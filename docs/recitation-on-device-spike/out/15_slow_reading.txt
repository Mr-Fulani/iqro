Slow reading, and the line that said "I can't hear you" to a loud reader. A Mac, the app's Dart code
(tool/recitation_model/harness.dart), recordings from everyayah.com made slower or faster with ffmpeg's atempo
(the pitch stays). 21.11.2026. "before": the long word rule of out/13_long_words.txt; "after": this change.

# The owner read 2:15 slowly, in his own voice: "tughyanihim" would not pass, and the line under the text said
# to read louder. Read fast it passed. Two causes of the first, found on recordings made slower:
#  1. A short vowel held. A slow reader holds his short vowels a little, and the model writes each as the short
#     vowel and its long one ("bi-i-him"). The frames of the long one were speech the text does not have; the
#     path went wrong and took the reading for a later word ("fi-i") begun instead: a word left out.
#     After: the long vowel right after its short one is more of the same sound - not where the text has that
#     long vowel there, and not for a sound a word only begins with after a stop.
#  2. A word judged before it ended. In the middle of a slow word the model hears wrongly for a while (a held
#     "ll" as "m") and puts it right as more of the word comes; the word was called a mistake a fifth of a second
#     after its last sound so far. After: sounds that do not fit are a mistake once the word is over - the next
#     word has begun, the reader is silent, or a second has gone by. Another letter heard for sure stops the
#     place at once, as before (waiting for it too cost three changed vowels of 150).
# The second: the line was shown whenever no word was taken for eight seconds, whatever the microphone heard.
#     After: "I can't hear you" only when the model writes next to no sounds for sure since the last word taken;
#     when it does, "I can hear you, but the word was not recognised".
